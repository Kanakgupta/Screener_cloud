⚖️ U.S. legal pathway — bank resolution (not Chapter 11)

  • Insured banks cannot be debtors under Title 11 of the U.S. Code (11 U.S.C. §109(b)(2)). A failing bank is closed by its chartering regulator and placed into FDIC receivership (12 U.S.C. §1821), usually over a weekend, with deposits moved to an acquirer.
  • Deposits are insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. Uninsured deposits and bondholders can take losses.
  • The listed bank holding company can file Chapter 11 after losing its bank (e.g. SVB Financial Group, March 2023); its shareholders are typically wiped out.
  • Early warning: Prompt Corrective Action (12 U.S.C. §1831o) restricts growth, dividends and management as capital ratios fall. Systemically important firms may be resolved under Dodd-Frank Title II.

Risk-management lens: bank failures are usually liquidity events (deposit runs) triggered by solvency doubts — concentrated depositors, unrealised securities losses, rapid growth and weak interest-rate risk management.

Methodology

Score = capped sum of rule-based red flags across seven dimensions (liquidity, solvency, profitability, cash flow, sales trend, market signal, news). Ohlson O-score (1980) gives an accounting-based 1-year failure probability; Merton distance-to-default (Bharath & Shumway 2008) gives a market-implied probability. Financial institutions use a capital-based rule set. Recalculated every trading day from the latest filings, prices and news.
